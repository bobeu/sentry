# Sentry Round 3 Review Questions

**Goal:** Force reviewers to engage with the product page itself, not just metadata. Test whether they can identify observable workflow elements and distinguish verifiable from unverifiable claims.

---

## Proposed Questions for Round 3

### Q1: What is the workflow Sentry describes for employers? (Freeform)
**Type:** Freeform text  
**Rationale:** Reviewers must read the "Simple flow, clear accountability" section. If they only scraped metadata, they cannot answer this specific question. A good answer will mention one or more of: Enable → Fund → Verify steps, configurable workflows, prepaid wallet, employer controls.  
**Will catch:** Reviewers who only read title/description will produce generic answers or fail to answer.

---

### Q2: Which of the following statements does this site claim it does NOT do? (Multiselect)
**Type:** Multiselect  
**Choices:**
- [ ] "Operate with hidden custody or undocumented wallet control"
- [ ] "Provide legal or fiduciary authority over user funds"
- [ ] "Require human employees to run the Telegram group"
- [ ] "Execute workflows without employer review"
- [ ] "Offer privacy guarantees not publicly disclosed"

**Correct answers:** First two, optionally third and fourth.  
**Rationale:** This question directly tests whether reviewers saw the "Observable facts" / "Not assumed" / "Avoided claims" section we added. It separates reviewers who engaged with the page from those who didn't.  
**Will catch:** Reviewers who missed the verifiability section.

---

### Q3: The site shows a three-step workflow (Enable → Fund → Verify). What is the purpose of the "Verify" step according to the page? (Freeform)
**Type:** Freeform text  
**Rationale:** This tests whether reviewers saw the explicit workflow section. The page states "The group sees actual outputs: answers, summaries, moderation, reports, and any wallet-linked event logs configured by the employer." Reviewers must extract this.  
**Will catch:** Reviewers who skimmed only the beginning and didn't scroll.

---

### Q4: List any four of the six capabilities shown in the "What Sentry can do" section or feature spotlight. (Freeform)
**Type:** Freeform text  
**Rationale:** Reviewers must identify actual product features from the page. Acceptable answers include: "Community Q&A", "Moderation", "Engagement", "Games & rewards", "Member hub", "Ops", "Employer", "Voice"; or from the hero section: "FAQ", "Moderation", "Reports", "Shift Handover", "Escalation", "Playbooks", "Roles", "Engagement & Rewards".  
**Will catch:** Reviewers who cannot name any actual feature and resort to generic phrasing.

---

### Q5: The site states it does not claim what kinds of authority over user funds? (Freeform)
**Type:** Freeform text  
**Rationale:** This directly probes the avoidance section. The page explicitly says "No hidden custody claim" and "No implied legal or financial fiduciary role." Reviewers must extract these exact limitations.  
**Will catch:** Reviewers who miss the entire "avoided claims" section.

---

### Q6: Is the product presented as autonomous, or does it require ongoing employer control and review? Provide one piece of evidence from the page to support your answer. (Freeform)
**Type:** Freeform text  
**Rationale:** This tests if reviewers understand the core product model. The page explicitly positions the employer as the decision-maker and controller. Evidence can be any of: the workflow diagram, "The employer funds the prepaid wallet..." copy, the playbooks section, the escalation rules mention, or the "enable or disable features in the dashboard" statement.  
**Will catch:** Reviewers who claim the product is autonomous or lacks employer controls (a key misunderstanding from Round 1 & 2).

---

### Q7: How would you rate the overall clarity of the product offering? (Rating: 1–10)
**Type:** Rating  
**Rationale:** This allows reviewers to give their own subjective assessment, but only after they've had to engage with the content answering Q1–Q6.

---

### Q8: What is the clearest strength of this product's public presentation? (Freeform)
**Type:** Freeform text  
**Rationale:** Open-ended, but requires reviewers to form an opinion based on their review of the page. Expect answers like: "transparent about limitations", "clear workflow diagram", "explicit about what it doesn't claim", "specific feature list", "employer controls are obvious".  
**Will catch:** Generic praise ("nice site", "good colors") will be obvious to the builder.

---

### Q9: What specific observable evidence does the site provide to demonstrate the product actually works? (Freeform)
**Type:** Freeform text  
**Rationale:** This tests whether reviewers can identify the simulated chat interface, dashboard screenshots, workflow diagrams, or other visual proof. If they only saw metadata, they cannot answer this.  
**Will catch:** Reviewers who didn't actually view the page or who claim "no observable evidence" when the page is full of it.

---

### Q10: Overall, does this site successfully explain what it does and what it does not claim? (Freeform)
**Type:** Freeform text  
**Rationale:** Summary question that requires a synthesis. Good answers will acknowledge both the clarity, the verifiability section, and the employer-control model. Poor answers will repeat generic complaints about "no visible content" (which contradicts the page).  
**Will catch:** Reviewers who give contradictory or template feedback.

---

## Meta notes for Round 3 submission

- **These questions force engagement** with the actual page content, not just metadata scraping
- **Q2 and Q5** directly test whether reviewers read the new "verifiable vs. unverifiable" section
- **Q1, Q3, Q4, Q6** probe specific page content that shallow reviewers cannot answer without viewing
- **Q9** is a "tell" question — if a reviewer says "no observable evidence", they either didn't visit the page or are using a bot that doesn't render HTML
- **Q7, Q8, Q10** allow subjective opinion but only after forced engagement

If Round 3 reviewers still give template-like answers or claim "no visible content" after these questions, the problem is clearly the reviewer bot, not your product.
